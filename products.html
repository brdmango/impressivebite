Fixed

Crown & Bridge

The bread and butter, treated like it matters.

Single units through long-span bridges, inlays, onlays and veneers-as-restorations. The material conversation is usually short: monolithic zirconia where strength and space are the constraints, lithium disilicate where it's going to be visible, and layered ceramics when the patient's expectations justify the extra stages.

Every unit is designed against your scan and shown to you before milling. We check contacts, occlusal clearance and margin fit digitally, and then a technician checks them again on the finished unit.

  • Shade matched from your photos — send them with the case
  • Contacts adjusted to your stated preference, tight or passive
  • Margin ridden to the scan, with a note back if the prep didn't capture cleanly
  • Printed model included when the case calls for one

Implant Restorations

Where getting the components right matters more than anything we could do downstream.

Screw-retained crowns, cement-retained crowns on custom abutments, and full-arch hybrids. Send the scan body data and tell us the implant system and platform — we confirm the components in writing before the case moves, because discovering a mismatch at seat is the worst possible time.

Full-arch work runs in stages with approval at each one. We'd rather spend an extra appointment on verification than deliver an arch that doesn't seat passively.

  • System and platform confirmed before any work starts
  • Custom abutments in titanium or zirconia
  • Emergence profile designed to your soft-tissue photos
  • Verification jigs for multi-unit cases on request

Scan bodies must be fully seated and clearly captured. A scan body that's even slightly off-axis produces a restoration that won't seat, and it isn't something we can correct in design.

Dentures & Partials

Digitally designed, which changes what happens the second time.

Full dentures, immediates, and partials in cast metal or flexible resin. The clinical stages are familiar; what's different is that the finished denture exists as a file. A patient who breaks one, loses one, or needs a spare doesn't start over — we reprint from the design that already fit.

Try-ins come back to you for patient approval before anything is finished, and we'll adjust and resend as many times as the case genuinely needs.

  • Full dentures, immediates and partials
  • Cast chrome-cobalt or flexible resin frameworks
  • Digital try-in, with the design kept on file
  • Reprints from the original file for repairs and spares
  • Repairs and relines on existing appliances

Night Guards & Splints

The case type most labs treat as filler. We don't.

A night guard is usually the first thing a lab lets slip when it's busy, and the patient is the one who notices. Ours run on a short fixed turnaround, and the occlusion is adjusted in the design so you're making minutes of chairside adjustments rather than grinding through an appointment.

Hard acrylic, soft, and dual-laminate for patients who can't tolerate a rigid surface. Michigan-style splints for cases where you're managing something more than wear.

  • Hard, soft and dual-laminate
  • Michigan and anterior deprogrammer splints
  • Occlusion balanced digitally against the opposing scan
  • Design kept on file for replacements

Anterior Esthetics

The cases where the patient should agree to the result before you prep.

Veneer cases, smile design, and the diagnostic work that goes in front of them. We start with a digital wax-up and a printed mock-up matrix, so the patient sees and approves the shape in their own mouth before a handpiece touches anything.

These cases take longer and are quoted individually, because the number of stages depends on how particular the patient is — which is impossible to know in advance and foolish to rush.

  • Digital wax-up from your scan and photos
  • Printed mock-up matrix for chairside try-in
  • Layered ceramics with characterization
  • Photography-guided shade matching
  • Stage-by-stage approval before anything is finished
