Scanners

If it exports a mesh, we can work from it

We're scanner-agnostic on purpose. Nobody should have to change hardware to change labs.

We work from the open formats every current intraoral scanner can produce — STL, PLY and DCM. Some scanners export these by default; some need open export switched on in settings, which is usually one toggle and occasionally a conversation with your scanner rep.

If you're not sure what yours does, put the model on the contact form. We'll tell your team exactly where the setting is, and if it turns out your scanner is locked to a closed ecosystem, we'll tell you that honestly rather than letting you find out mid-case.

No scanner at all? That's fine. Send PVS impressions with a prepaid label and we digitize them here — the case then runs through exactly the same workflow, with the same design approval step.

Photos matter more than people expect on anterior work. A shade tab held in the same frame as the adjacent teeth, in natural light, is worth more than any written shade note.

Submitting a case

From the operatory to the lab queue

The whole submission should take about two minutes. If it's taking longer than that, tell us — it means we've designed something badly.

Upload the files

Drag the exported scan into the case portal. Multiple files are fine; we'd rather have the extra arch than discover we need it tomorrow.

Fill the Rx

Tooth numbers, restoration type, material, shade, and your due date. Anything you'd normally write on a paper slip goes here instead.

Add a note to the technician

Optional, and the most useful field on the form. "Patient hates bulky contacts," "the #19 margin is subgingival on the distal," "this one's a perfectionist" — all of it changes what we do.

Get your delivery date

We review the case the same business day, confirm it's workable, and give you a delivery date. If something in the scan is a problem, that's when you hear about it — not after it's been sitting in a queue.

Case review happens before the queue

Every case is looked at by a person when it lands. We're checking that margins are readable, the bite makes sense, the opposing arch is there, and scan bodies are seated.

If something's wrong, you hear within the same business day, while the patient is still fresh in your mind and a rescan is a phone call rather than a disruption.

Design approval

For anything that seats on a prep, we send the proposed design before milling. You see margins, contacts, occlusal clearance and emergence.

Approve it and we mill. Send it back with a note and we revise — there's no limit and no charge for revisions at the design stage, because that's the cheapest possible place to fix something.

Standing approval is available if you'd rather not review routine single units. Plenty of practices do this once they've seen a few come back.

Turnaround

What the numbers actually mean

Turnaround is in-lab working days — from case acceptance to the unit leaving us. Transit is separate, and we quote it separately, because a lab that folds shipping into its "turnaround" is hiding something.

Scan quality

What makes a scan we can work from

None of this is new to you. It's here because it's what we send back cases for, and a shared list is easier than a phone call.

Common scan problems and their fixes
What we see What it causes What fixes it
Margin obscured by tissue or blood We guess the finish line, and the crown doesn't seat Retract and achieve hemostasis before scanning; a second cord pass beats a remake
Insufficient occlusal reduction Restoration is under-contoured or perforates Check clearance in excursion before scanning; we'll flag it, but earlier is better
Bite scan doesn't match the arches Occlusion is wrong on delivery Scan the bite in maximum intercuspation with the patient fully closed, and verify on screen
Missing opposing arch Case can't start Send both arches even when it feels unnecessary; it costs nothing
Scan body not fully seated Implant restoration won't seat, and it can't be fixed in design Confirm the scan body is fully engaged and verify radiographically when in doubt
Scan stitched across a large edentulous span Accumulated distortion across the arch Add scan aids or landmarks across the span; consider a physical impression for full arches
No shade photo on an anterior case Shade is matched to a written note and misses One photo with a shade tab beside the adjacent tooth, in natural light, no flash

Shipping

  • Digital cases upload — nothing physical moves and nothing can get lost
  • Prepaid labels sent to your office for impressions and anything returning to us
  • Tracked outbound shipping with the tracking number on the case
  • Shipping to all 50 states

We don't charge for inbound shipping. Making a practice pay to send us work has always been a strange way to run a service business.

Remakes & adjustments

If a restoration doesn't seat and the cause is on our side, we remake it, cover shipping both ways, and move it ahead of new work.

If the cause is in the scan or the prep, we'll show you — the file open, the specific problem on screen — and remake it at a reduced rate so the patient isn't caught in the middle of a disagreement about fault.

Send the case number and what you saw at seat. We don't require the restoration back before we start the remake.

Standards & materials

What we'll put in writing

  • Restorations designed and finished by licensed dental technicians
  • Materials sourced from established manufacturers, with lot traceability on request
  • Material safety and composition documentation available for any case
  • Case files and designs retained, so remakes and replacements start from original data
  • Patient information handled in line with your practice's HIPAA obligations

Need documentation for a specific case or material — a certificate of origin, a material composition sheet, an invoice breakdown for insurance? Ask and we'll send it.

For licensed dental professionals. Everything on this page is guidance for working with our lab and doesn't replace your own clinical judgment, or the manufacturer's instructions for any material used chairside.
